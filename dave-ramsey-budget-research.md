# Dave Ramsey Budgeting Categories & Percentages — Research Note

Research note (Aug 8, 2026). Compiled from Ramsey Solutions primary sources only (ramseysolutions.com). Sections labeled **Ramsey guidance** are directly sourced; sections labeled **Analysis / inference** are my own synthesis and should be read as such. The findings were applied to `categories.md` while preserving the original teaching-oriented categories.

## TL;DR

- Ramsey publishes **very few hard percentages**: giving **10%**, housing **≤25% of take-home**, and retirement investing **15% of household income (Baby Step 4 only)**. For food, utilities, and transportation Ramsey explicitly says there is **no set percentage** — budget on actual spending.
- The percentage ranges in `categories.md` (Saving 10–15%, Housing 25–30%, Utilities 5–10%, Food 10–15%) **do not come from Ramsey**. Only Housing's 25% is close, and Ramsey's is a *ceiling* (≤25%), not a range.
- Ramsey's budget structure is an **ordered priority list**, not a list of percentages: Giving → Saving → Four Walls (food, utilities, shelter, transportation) → other essentials (insurance, debt, childcare) → nonessentials + a small Miscellaneous line.
- **Sinking funds** are a core Ramsey mechanism for irregular expenses: save a set amount monthly toward a *known, dated* future cost (tires, Christmas, car repairs, vacation). Sinking funds are distinct from the emergency fund (known vs. unknown).
- For **irregular income**, Ramsey's rule is: budget on your *lowest* recent month's income, prioritize essentials, adjust every paycheck.

## Official category structure (Ramsey guidance)

Ramsey frames categories as a **priority order** to fill top-down in a zero-based budget (income − every planned expense = $0), keeping a **$100–300 bank buffer**:

1. **Giving** — tithing and charitable giving (10%).
2. **Saving** — emergency fund (by Baby Step), retirement (15% at BS4), sinking funds / large purchases.
3. **Four Walls** — **food**, **utilities**, **shelter/housing**, **transportation**. (Only groceries, not restaurants, are an essential.)
4. **Other essentials** — insurance, debt payments, childcare.
5. **Nonessentials** — entertainment, dining out, subscriptions, personal spending / fun money, plus a **Miscellaneous** line for the unexpected.

This ordering is consistent across the Budgeting hub FAQ, the How-to-Budget guide, the irregular-income article, and Rachel Cruze's monthly-expenses article.

## Sinking funds (Ramsey guidance — Rachel Cruze, updated Mar 20, 2025)

- **Definition**: "A strategic way to save money by setting aside a little bit of money each month" for a specific, known future expense.
- **Examples Ramsey gives**: new tires, Christmas gifts, vet bills, wedding expenses, plane tickets, birthday parties, school books and supplies, clothes for a special occasion, vacations, home remodels, concert tickets.
- **Sinking fund vs. emergency fund**: sinking fund = *known* upcoming expense you plan for; emergency fund = *unknown* urgent expense. "Use sinking funds for everything else" (non-emergencies).
- **How to create one (4 steps)**: (1) decide what you're saving for; (2) decide where to hold it (separate savings account with no minimum balance, or track in EveryDollar under the **Savings category**); (3) total cost ÷ months until you need it = monthly amount (e.g., $1,000 Christmas over 3 months ≈ $330/mo); (4) put it as a line item in the monthly budget.
- **How many?** Focus on "just a few things at a time" given limited monthly cash; it's about choosing what to prioritize.
- The How-to-Budget guide frames these under "month-specific expenses to prep for": celebrations, holidays, seasonal purchases (back-to-school), semiannual expenses (insurance premiums), annual expenses (memberships).

## Irregular income (Ramsey guidance — updated Mar 21, 2025)

- Budget using your **lowest** monthly income from the past few months, not an average.
- Fill expense categories in priority order (giving → saving → Four Walls → essentials → nonessentials + miscellaneous), and be ready to skip nonessentials.
- Make **adjustments every paycheck**: add any extra income to the budget (toward a money goal), and cut nonessentials if income comes in low.
- There is an official **Irregular Income Budget Planning form (PDF)** linked from the article.

## Gaps & discrepancies vs. categories.md (Analysis / inference — not Ramsey's words)

- **Percentages**: `categories.md` labels Saving 10–15%, Housing 25–30%, Utilities 5–10%, Food 10–15%. Ramsey only endorses giving 10%, housing **≤25%** (a cap), and retirement 15% (BS4). The Food/Utilities ranges and "Saving 10–15%" are not Ramsey guidance.
- **Giving should be its own top category.** `categories.md` nests giving under "Saving" and "Family and Giving"; Ramsey makes Giving the first, separate 10% category.
- **No explicit "Kids" category.** Ramsey has a Kids category (childcare, school expenses, sports/extracurriculars); `categories.md` scatters these across Education and Family and Giving.
- **No "Month-Specific" bucket** in `categories.md` for one-off/irregular months (taxes, holidays, gifts, renewals) — though its intro does describe sinking funds.
- **Missing explicit lines**: Tithing, identity theft protection (Insurance), fun money as a defined personal line, and the **$100–300 bank buffer** guidance.
- **Sinking-fund framing**: `categories.md` lists sinking-fund-like goals under "Saving" and introduces sinking funds, but doesn't formalize Ramsey's rule that a sinking fund is a *specific, dated* goal line in the Savings category (and that only a few should run at once).

- I treated the Budget Calculator's FAQ and the hub FAQ as authoritative percentage sources; Ramsey publishes no single "official percentage chart," so figures had to be assembled across several pages.

- The resulting `categories.md` intentionally includes additional teaching-oriented lines beyond Ramsey's official list, such as bicycle maintenance, work expenses, and broader household costs.
